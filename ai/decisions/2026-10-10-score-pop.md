# The score pop is a separate `aria-hidden` element, one at a time, and it fades in place under reduced motion

Date: 2026-10-10 · Status: accepted

## Context

A move that merges tiles shows the points it brought as a short `+N` that rises from the
score and fades (MY-124). Three choices had an obvious alternative:

- **Where it sits next to the score's live region.** The score is announced through a
  hidden, debounced live region and its visible number is `aria-hidden`
  (`2026-10-10-score-live-region-and-focus-ring.md`); a `+N` that a screen reader could
  reach would be a second, unrequested announcement.
- **What a burst of moves does.** The move cooldown is 100 ms and the pop lasts 600 ms,
  so a held key can start a pop while up to five earlier ones are still in the air.
- **What reduced motion does.** The global rule in `motion.css` makes every animation
  0.01 ms long, which would erase a pop at once.

## Decision

- **A separate element.** `ScoreView.showScoreGain(points)` puts a `<span class="score-pop">`
  into `.score-pops`, an empty container in the `Score` box with `aria-hidden="true"`.
  It is outside the live region and outside `.current-score`: the announcement and its
  debounce are untouched, and the visible value's text stays the bare number. Nothing is
  shown for a gain of 0 (a slide), and `start()` and the restored save never call it.
- **Each pop is its own element, removed on `animationend`.** No JS timer and no JS
  copy of the duration: it is a CSS custom property (`--score-pop-duration`, 600 ms)
  read by the animation and by the reduced-motion override. Rejected: reusing one
  element and restarting its animation (needs a forced reflow, and cuts the previous pop
  short).
- **One pop at a time.** A new pop replaces the one still in the air
  (`replaceChildren`), so the player always reads the points of the last move. Tried and
  rejected: letting them coexist. As in-flow blocks they stack into a column up to six
  lines (about 130 px) tall that climbs over the logo row, and each new pop pushes the
  older ones up a line; absolutely positioned they would sit 5 to 8 px apart with
  different numbers on top of one another, and with no movement at all (reduced motion)
  exactly on top of one another. Replacing also bounds the DOM: even if `animationend`
  never fired there would be one pop, not a pile. The pop starts at full opacity, with no
  fade-in, so a held key does not make the text blink.
- **It floats out of the layout** (`position: absolute` on the container,
  `pointer-events: none`), at the right edge of the score value, and rises about 28 px
  inside the box: the header never shifts and the pop never takes a tap. (Where it sits
  and how far it rises changed later: see the amendment at the end.)
- **Reduced motion (the author's decision): the `+N` still shows, without movement.**
  It appears in place and fades out over the same 600 ms. `motion.css` overrides the
  global rule for `.score-pop` only: `--score-pop-rise: 0px` and the duration restored
  with `!important`; the more specific selector beats `*`.

## Consequences

- A pop in the air when `New Game` or the game-over dialog's button is pressed finishes by
  itself (at most 600 ms). Clearing it would need a controller call for a rare case.
- Another transient decoration that must stay visible under reduced motion follows the
  same pattern: its own override in `motion.css`, never an exemption in the global rule.
- Not verified without a real screen reader: that NVDA and VoiceOver never read the
  pop. The accessibility tree (no `+N` node) and the live-region text (only the bare
  number, once) were checked.
- Reversing "one at a time" is `append` instead of `replaceChildren`; the stacked
  layout then has to be decided.

## Amendment (2026-10-10, MY-123): the pop sits in the label row, above the digits

- **What was wrong.** The pop was placed at the right edge of the score value, level with
  the digits. Rubik's wider digits (MY-121) make a 6-digit score fill the Score box, so at
  320px and 375px the pop landed on the last digits (`123456` with `+32768` drawn over it).
  Measured over 480 combinations of theme, width (320, 375, 1000), motion setting, score
  (6 and 7 digits), pop (`+4` to `+65536`) and moment in the animation: 182 overlapped the
  digit text, 98 of them at 320px and 84 at 375px, none at 1000px. Scores of 6 digits are
  reachable in normal play.
- **New placement.** The pop is at the right end of the **label row**, above the digits
  (the `SCORE` label is short, so the row is free), its bottom edge 2px above the digits'
  box, and it rises 6px (it was 28px, which would now reach the logo row: the gap between
  the rows is 10px at 320px). Its line height is 1. Reduced motion is unchanged: no rise,
  fades in place.
- **How, in CSS only.** `.score-pops` is a zero-size grid item in row 1, column 2 of the
  score box, aligned to the end of the row, so its bottom edge is exactly the line between
  the label row and the digits whatever the font size or the number of digits; `.score-pop`
  is absolutely positioned from it (`right: 0; bottom: 2px`). It still takes no space, so the
  header never shifts, and it needed no JS: `ScoreView` is unchanged. The label got an
  explicit `grid-row: 1` so the anchor in the same row does not push it down.
- **Measured after.** In all 480 combinations the pop is at least 2px from the digits (the
  element box and the text), 21px from the label, 12px from the logo, 70px from the toolbar
  and 23px from the High Score box; no layout shift; the pop box pokes at most 0.8px above
  the Score box while it is visible (empty line-box space, the glyphs are inside), 2px at the
  end of the rise when it is nearly transparent. A real merge through `ScoreView` gives the
  same, with and without reduced motion, and the pop still removes itself on `animationend`.
- **Rejected.** Moving the pop above the digits only at narrow widths (two layouts and a
  breakpoint to keep, when the label row works at every width); a smaller pop (it still
  collides at 7 digits); measuring the digits in JS to place the pop (the point of the
  design is that JS holds no geometry); keeping the old rise (it would cover the logo).
