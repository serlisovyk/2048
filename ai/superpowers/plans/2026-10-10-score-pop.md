## 1. Choices

### Each pop is its own element, created and removed by `ScoreView`

`ScoreView` owns the score, so it owns the pop (`ai/rules/oop.md`: the view that owns the
data owns how it looks). `showScoreGain(points)` creates a `<span class="score-pop">+N</span>`,
puts it into a container, and removes it on `animationend` (a `{ once: true }` listener on
that element). No timer, no forced reflow to restart an animation on a reused element, so
no JS timing and nothing new in `constants.js`: the duration lives in CSS only
(`--score-pop-duration`), because JS never needs to know it.

The model already reports the points of a move: `Game.move()` returns `scoreGained`
(the sum of all merges in the move). `GameController.requestMove` passes it on right after
`renderScore()`; `ScoreView` shows nothing for `0`. `start()` and the restore path never
call it, so `New Game` and a restored save are silent without any extra condition.

### One pop at a time (departs from the issue's wording — for the author to confirm)

The issue says a series of moves must not pile up into a mess, "each pop its own element
with its own animation". Letting those elements coexist cannot satisfy the first part.
The cooldown is 100 ms and the pop lasts 600 ms, so up to six pops would be in the air.
Absolutely positioned, all starting at the same point, they would sit only 5 to 8px apart
(a rise of about 28px) on top of one another, with numbers that differ (`+4`, `+8`,
`+4`): unreadable, and under reduced motion exactly on top of one another. As in-flow
blocks in the container they stack into a column instead: tried in the browser, five
pops are a column about 110px tall that climbs out of the box over the logo row, and
each new pop pushes the older ones up a line.

So a pop that arrives while another is in the air replaces it (`replaceChildren(pop)`):
the newest `+N` is always readable, and the earlier pop is dropped. Each pop is still its
own element with its own animation, and removed by `animationend` (or by the next pop).
A side effect that is worth having: even if `animationend` never fired, the DOM would
hold one pop, not a growing pile. If the author prefers coexisting pops, the change is
`append` instead of `replaceChildren`, and the column has to be accepted or redesigned.

The pop starts at full opacity, with no fade-in: a replaced pop would otherwise make the
text blink off and on in a held-key burst.

### Where it sits

A small empty `<span class="score-pops" aria-hidden="true">` at the end of the `Score`
box (after the live-region span) is the container. `aria-hidden` on the container hides
every pop at once; the container is not inside the live region and not inside
`.current-score`, so `textContent` of the score and the announcement never see it.

`.score-box` becomes `position: relative`; the container is `position: absolute` at the
box's bottom-right corner (`right: 12px`, the box's own padding; `bottom: 10px`, so that
the pop's baseline sits level with the score digits' baseline), so it sits at the right
edge of the value row, where the `Score` box has nothing (the value is left-aligned, and
the second grid column holds nothing in this box). A pop is a block inside it (a
`transform` does not apply to an inline box). Out-of-flow, `pointer-events: none`: no layout shift, and a pop never takes a
click. The score box has no `overflow: hidden`, and the rise (about 28px) stays inside the
box (it is 60 to 65px tall), so nothing is clipped.

A narrow board (320px) leaves about 119px of content width in a box; a six-digit score
(about 72px) and `+2048` at 14px (about 40px) fit side by side. A longer pop over a
longer score would overlap for the moments it exists; that is not reachable in practice.

### Animation and reduced motion

One keyframes rule: opacity holds 1 for the first 60% and falls to 0, `translateY` goes
from `0` to `var(--score-pop-rise, -28px)`, `ease-out`, `forwards` (so it does not flash
back to opacity 1 before the element is removed). Colour is the gold of the score digits,
bold, `clamp(14px, 4vw, 18px)`.

`motion.css` holds a global rule that sets every `animation-duration` to `0.01ms
!important`. Left alone, it would end the pop at once and the author's decision (the
`+N` still shows, without movement) could not be met. So the reduced-motion block gets one
rule: `.score-pop { --score-pop-rise: 0px; animation-duration: var(--score-pop-duration)
!important }`. Two `!important` declarations: the more specific one wins (`.score-pop`,
0,1,0, beats `*`, 0,0,0). The same keyframes then fade in place. `--score-pop-duration`
is set once on `.score-pops` and read by both the base rule and the override, so 600 ms is
not written twice.

### A pop still in the air at `New Game` or game over

It finishes on its own (at most 600 ms). Clearing it would need another public method and
a call from the controller for a rare case: a click on a dialog button within 600 ms of
the move that opened the dialog. Left as is; recorded here so it is a known edge, not a
miss.

## 4. Verification

`yarn lint`, `yarn format:check`, `yarn build`, `yarn size` (baseline from `main`: JS
4.25 kB of 8 kB, CSS 2.34 kB of 4 kB gzip), then play it in `yarn dev --port 5192
--strictPort`. The built-in browser pane is hidden here (animations do not advance and
`animationend` may never fire), so the visual checks run in a separate headless Chrome
driven over the DevTools protocol (real key and touch events, emulated reduced motion).
Boards with merges ready are set up through `localStorage.savedGame`.

- **Right number**: a board where one move merges `2+2` and `4+4` shows `+12` and the
  score goes up by 12; `+4`, `+16`, `+128` single merges.
- **Nothing**: a plain slide, a blocked move, `New Game`, a restored save, and the page
  load leave `.score-pops` empty.
- **Burst**: key presses 100 ms apart on a board that merges every move: never more than
  one `.score-pop` in the container; after the burst and 600 ms the container has no
  children. A screenshot in the middle of the burst shows one readable number.
- **Header**: at 320px and at desktop width, the bounding boxes of the logo, the toolbar,
  and both score boxes are identical before a move, with a pop in the air, and after it;
  swipes at 320px (touch events) work and the page does not scroll sideways.
- **Reduced motion** (`Emulation.setEmulatedMedia`): the pop is visible and fades, its
  `transform` stays at no translation for its whole life, and it is removed after
  about 600 ms.
- **Screen reader**: the pop and its container are absent from the accessibility tree
  (`aria-hidden`); the live region text changes to the bare score, once, 500 ms after the
  last move, and never to `+N`. No NVDA or VoiceOver pass is possible here and is
  reported as manual.
- Screenshots of the pop (desktop, 320px, reduced motion) go to the scratchpad.
